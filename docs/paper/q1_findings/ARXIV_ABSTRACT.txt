Runtime middleware for LLM agents maps detector scores to discrete actions (sanitize, restrict tools, block) and may adapt intervention level over episodes under a utility cost. Whether label-blind, cost-aware policies reduce defense-attributed attack success while preserving benign utility requires hash-locked packs, an independent judge (Target not equal to Judge), and pre-registered stop rules—not a design narrative.

We report dual-track confirmatory evaluation on two disjoint frozen packs and treatments. Track A (VNEXT): on vnext_confirm_v1.0 (61 attack + 61 benign), VNEXT-ADAPT vs B0 FAILS the pre-registered qualified win: McNemar b10=5, b01=0, p=0.0625 (not significant at alpha=0.05); paired effect 0.0820 below MSID 0.20; utility U=0.9344 below gate 0.95. Track B (Phase-1): on a separate pack phase1_confirm_v1, PHASE1-CORE vs B0 shows scoped SUPPORTED_IMPROVEMENT under Phase-1 MSID with utility eligible (effect 0.4426, 95% CI [0.2757, 0.6096] per AUDIT)—a different treatment and corpus; it is not a VNEXT pass and does not overturn Track A FAIL.

A closed Layer A diagnostic (40+40 TEST) shows detector lift without a significant adaptive ASR reduction (McNemar p=0.125). The contribution is honest evaluation methodology and a frozen negative/positive pair under explicit claim boundaries—not a fielded guard, SOTA defense, or solution to prompt injection.
