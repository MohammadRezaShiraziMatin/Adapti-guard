# Q1 F3 findings: does adaptation help? (real LLM)

Generated by `scripts/q1_f3_adaptive_vs_fixed.py` (full tables in `SUMMARY.md`, per-run in `runs.json`, per-episode in `episodes.jsonl`). 2 target models via OpenRouter (llama-3.1-8b-instruct, gpt-4o-mini), 2 stream types (uniform 25% attacks; burst 10%/40%/5%), 5 seeds, 150 episodes per run, temperature 0.3, 0 API errors.

## Design (non-circular)
- Attack success is a programmatic check on the **model response** (canary leak or injected marker), never the detector. Utility is a keyword check on benign responses.
- 28 hand-written synthetic attacks (8 detector-friendly direct leaks, 8 evasive direct leaks, 8 document-embedded injections, 4 direct marker asks) and 20 benign prompts, including 4 suspicious-looking legitimate ones. These are **not** the frozen packs and were not tuned against the detector; nothing frozen was re-run.
- `adaptive_oracle` is fed ground-truth episode type plus the response check, i.e. an upper bound on feedback quality. `adaptive_proxy` is the historical `AdaptiveDefenseState` (detector-derived feedback, one-way ratchet). `adaptive_proxy_streak` adds the new benign-streak de-escalation.

## Results (paired against fixed_l1, mean over seeds, bootstrap 95% CI over 5 seeds)
- **No arm shows adaptation beating the best fixed level.** On gpt-4o-mini fixed_l2 (ASR 0.134, utility 0.940, cost 0.122) is better than every adaptive arm (ASR 0.15-0.21, utility 0.91-0.95, cost 0.10-0.15) on ASR and cost at equal or higher utility. On llama, fixed_l3 (ASR 0.166-0.212) is at least as secure as the adaptive arms (0.20-0.30) at only ~0.01 higher cost.
- Adaptive arms mostly sit between fixed_l1 and fixed_l3. `adaptive_oracle` lowers ASR vs fixed_l1 in 3 of 4 settings (e.g. gpt uniform ΔASR -0.151 [-0.175,-0.127]; llama burst CI includes 0) but costs ~0.03-0.05 more and loses 0.02-0.03 utility.
- `adaptive_proxy_streak` is the cheapest adaptive arm (cost 0.099-0.117, equal to fixed_l1) and de-escalates (downs 5.4-6.0 per run), but its ASR advantage over fixed_l1 vanishes on llama (ΔASR +0.005 / +0.034, CIs include 0) and is smaller on gpt (-0.082 to -0.135).
- The old controller (`adaptive_proxy`) never de-escalates (downs 0.0) and ends at L3 in all four settings, confirming F1 on a real model.

## A finding about the defense levels, not the controller
For gpt-4o-mini, MEDIUM-risk attacks that are SANITIZEd (A1) succeed 36% of the time (56/155, fixed_l0), versus 0% (0/155) when passed raw (A2, no tool channel here). L1 therefore gives **no** ASR improvement over L0 (0.304 vs 0.304) and on llama is slightly worse (0.256 vs 0.226, not significant). A plausible mechanism is that the sanitizer deletes the trigger phrase ("ignore previous instructions") and leaves the injected request looking innocuous; this was not isolated. It means L2's apparent protection here is a pass-through artefact, not a defense.

## Limits (read before citing)
- 5 seeds and 150 episodes: CIs are wide, and bootstrap over 5 seeds is coarse. Seeds change the stream; the LLM sampling noise is small at temperature 0.3.
- Synthetic probes, 2 cheap models, text-only (no tools), so A2 TOOL_RESTRICTION is a no-op on content.
- The `adaptive_oracle` arm uses oracle feedback; a deployable judge would be worse.
- Keyword utility check is crude (fixed_l0 utility is 0.89-0.90 on llama because the model sometimes omits the keyword).
- This is exploratory, not confirmatory; it was not preregistered.
