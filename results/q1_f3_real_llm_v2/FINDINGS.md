# Q1 F3 v2: does adaptation help after the redesign? (real LLM)

Generated by `scripts/q1_f3_adaptive_vs_fixed_v2.py` (tables in `SUMMARY.md`, runs in `runs.json`, episodes in `episodes.jsonl`). 2 models via OpenRouter (llama-3.1-8b-instruct, gpt-4o-mini), 2 stream types, **5 seeds**, 150 episodes/run, temperature 0.3, 120 runs, 0 API errors. Arms: fixed L0-L3 and two adaptive arms on the **new stack** (hardened detector, delimiting sanitizer, controller with dwell 5 / back-off cap 4 / benign streak 10 at experiment scale). `adaptive_oracle` is fed ground-truth episode type plus a response check (upper bound on feedback); `adaptive_proxy` uses detector-derived feedback.

Not run, for budget (the key had $1.49 left): the legacy-stack arms and 3 of the planned 8 seeds. v1 results (`../q1_f3_real_llm/`) are the legacy-stack reference, but they used a different, smaller attack pool, so v1 and v2 numbers are not directly comparable.

Attack pool: 28 v1 attacks (the hardened patterns were designed while looking at these) plus 24 v2 attacks written without consulting the detector. v2 numbers on the v2 pool are the honest generalisation test.

> Update: later work (layered detector, offline replay, confirmatory F3 with a frozen contract) is in `docs/ADAPTIVE_CONTROLLER_CASE_STUDY.md`; the confirmatory result is inconclusive. This file is left as originally written.

## Headline: adaptive still does not beat the best fixed level
Paired per-seed differences, 95% bootstrap CI over 5 seeds (wide):

- **llama:** no arm dominates. Fixed L3 has the lowest ASR (0.000-0.006) but costs 0.164-0.174 and utility 0.80. Adaptive arms have ASR 0.015-0.031 (+0.015 to +0.025 vs L3, CIs exclude 0) at slightly better utility (+0.02 to +0.04, mixed CIs) and lower cost (-0.006 to -0.027). Versus fixed L2 (ASR 0.043-0.063, utility 0.84-0.89, cost 0.124-0.128), adaptive_oracle lowers ASR by 0.029-0.033 (burst CI touches 0) but costs +0.022 to +0.040 and does not improve utility. This is a trade-off curve, not a win.
- **gpt-4o-mini:** fixed L1 dominates the adaptive arms. ASR 0.143-0.144 vs 0.125-0.148 (differences within CI), utility 1.000 vs 0.971-0.985 (adaptive loses 0.015-0.029, CI excludes 0), cost 0.100 vs 0.137-0.165. Adaptive does weakly beat fixed L3 on this model (similar ASR, utility +0.005 to +0.022, cost -0.008 to -0.027).
- The adaptive arms de-escalate now (downs 1.2-2.4 per run, v1 `adaptive_proxy` had 0) and no longer cycle, but spend most episodes at L2-L3, so they pay most of L3's cost.

## What actually limits security here: the detector, not the controller
- Detector recall on the **v1 pool 0.943** but on the **fresh v2 pool only 0.258**; 3.5% of benign prompts are flagged. The hardened patterns generalise poorly, as expected of regex matching tuned on a known set.
- ASR on the v2 pool for gpt-4o-mini is 0.30-0.36 at **every** level including L3 (0.315): L3 only blocks what the detector flags, and the controller can only act on what the detector (or the oracle) sees. On the v1 pool gpt-4o-mini ASR is about 0.00-0.01 everywhere.
- Raising the level cannot fix a detector miss; no controller change can.

## Sanitizer
- Delimiting helps llama on the v2 pool: ASR 0.109 (L0, raw) -> 0.027 (L1) -> 0.004 (L2). L1 and L2 send identical content in `delimit` mode, so the L1/L2 gap (about 2-3 attacks) is sampling noise, not a level effect.
- On gpt-4o-mini the effect is small and not significant (v2 pool 0.363 -> 0.302 at L1; 0.363 -> 0.315 at L2). The v1 finding that stripping raised ASR is not reproducible here because v1 pool and sanitizer both changed; this run does not isolate it.

## Limits (read before citing)
- 5 seeds, 150 episodes, two cheap models, synthetic text-only probes, no adaptive attacker, keyword utility check. Exploratory, not preregistered.
- The oracle arm is an upper bound on feedback quality.
- Experiment-scale controller settings (streak 10, dwell 5) are shorter than the runtime defaults (20/10).
- HIGH risk is never produced by the detector-only pipeline, so that part of the policy was not exercised.
